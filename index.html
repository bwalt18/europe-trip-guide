Rome

12–16 Oct · 4 nights

Four days is enough for the big three plus real wandering. Mon–Fri means thinner crowds than a weekend. Nearly everything inside the walls is walkable — you'll use the metro maybe twice a day. City tax €4–6 per person per night on top of the room, usually cash at checkout.

More Rome — the ones people miss
Basilica di San Clemente — three churches stacked on top of each other: 12th-century over 4th-century over a Roman house with a Mithras temple, an underground stream still running. 45 min, 200 m from the Colosseum. The best "layers of Rome" thing you can do 📍€10
Castel Sant'Angelo — Hadrian's tomb turned papal fortress; the ramparts have the best sunset view over the river 📍€16

Baths of Caracalla — vast ruined imperial baths, walls still eight storeys high, almost nobody there 📍€8

Sorrento

16–18 Oct · 2 nights

A cliff-top town facing Vesuvius across the bay, and the perfect base — Pompeii, Herculaneum and Naples are all on one train line. Two nights is tight, so pick your battles: Pompeii on the 17th is non-negotiable, then either Naples or Herculaneum, not both.

Pompeii — the big one
Entry, "Pompeii Express" — book official. Named ticket — carry your passport. Timed slots, 20,000/day cap; book a 9am slot by late Sept 📍€20
"Pompeii+" — adds the Villa of the Mysteries and its fresco cycle, the best-preserved Roman painting anywhere. Worth the extra €5€25
Official 1.5-hour guided tour on top of entry — good value versus €35–55 private tours+€8
Train from Sorrento to "Pompei Scavi – Villa dei Misteri", ~30 min, station is 100 m from the gate~€3

Open 9:00–19:30, last entry 17:30. Allow 4–6 hours — it's a whole city, 66 hectares, with almost no shade. Proper shoes, hat, water (there are free ancient fountains to refill at). Bags bigger than 30×30×15 cm go in the cloakroom.

Day trips from Sorrento
Naples — train to Garibaldi (~70 min). Walk Spaccanapoli and Via dei Tribunali, free. Watch your bag near the station 📍€5
Cappella Sansevero, Naples — the Veiled Christ: one block of marble carved to look like wet cloth over a body. Book ahead, it sells out 📍€10
MANN museum, Naples — where the actual Pompeii mosaics and frescoes live. Closed Tuesdays 📍€20
Pio Monte della Misericordia, Naples — Caravaggio's Seven Works of Mercy, still hanging in the chapel it was painted for. Two minutes from Sansevero 📍€10
Herculaneum — smaller than Pompeii but far better preserved: wooden doors, upper floors, carbonised furniture. Fewer people too. Check the train stops at Ercolano Scavi 📍€16
Mount Vesuvius crater — walk to the rim and look in. DIY: train to Pompeii + EAV bus 808 (€5.40 return) + entry. Book a timed slot 📍€10
Napoli Sotterranea — 40 m under the city: Greek aqueduct, WWII shelter, a hidden Roman theatre. Guided, ~90 min 📍€10–15
In Sorrento itself — free & cheap
  • Vallone dei Mulini — a 900-year-old flour mill abandoned in a gorge, now swallowed by ferns. Visible from the street off Piazza Tasso. Free, 5 minutes, best photo in town. 📍
  • Marina Grande — the fishing village below the cliffs, with the best seafood in town at half the Piazza Tasso price. 10 min downhill. 📍
  • Bagni della Regina Giovanna — a natural sea pool inside a ruined Roman villa, 30–40 min walk west. Free, and still swimmable in October. 📍
  • Villa Comunale gardens at sunset — Vesuvius going pink across the water. Free.

Positano

18–20 Oct · 2 nights

Pastel houses stacked down a cliff to a grey-sand beach. It's a town built of stairs — there are no roads to the waterfront, so whatever you're carrying, you're carrying it down. The reward is that late October empties it out while the sea is still 22°C.

ℹ Porters are a real thing here Blue-shirted porters at the dock and bus stops carry bags to your accommodation for €10–20 each. With stairs like these it's money well spent — just agree the price before they pick anything up.
Getting there — 18 Oct

Ferry from Sorrento €16–21.5030–40 min, and you arrive at the beach rather than 200 stairs above it. Season runs to early November so you're fine. Book on Ferryhopper about a week out.

Or the SITA bus €2.6050–60 min on a spectacular, nauseating road. Stops at Chiesa Nuova or Sponda, both high above town. Buy at a tabacchi — you cannot buy on board.

Do — mostly free
  • Path of the Gods — free, and the best walk on the coast. Bus to Amalfi (€2.60), change for Bomerano (~€2), then ~3 hours along the cliff edge to Nocelle with the sea 500 m below you. Walk it east to west so it's downhill with the view ahead. Then 1,700 steps down, or the local bus (€1.75). 📍 trailhead
  • Santa Maria Assunta — the majolica-tiled dome you've seen in every photo; inside is a Byzantine Black Madonna. Free. 📍
  • Fornillo beach — 10 minutes on the coastal path from the main beach, quieter and with free sand. 📍
  • Via dei Mulini down to the beach — bougainvillea, ceramics, linen shops. The classic wander.
  • Swim — the sea is ~22°C. Beach clubs have packed up by now, but the free stretches are open and empty.
Experiences worth booking

  • Half-day group boat from €50 from A$85About four hours. The cheapest way to see Positano from the water, which is the view the town was built for.
  • Sunset cruise €73 A$124Crapolla, 1.5 hours with a prosecco aperitivo out past Li Galli. Sunset is ~6:15pm, so expect a 5pm departure. Runs to 9 Nov.
  • Private charter €650 per boat A$1,105Lucibello, half day, skipper and fuel and champagne included. Per boat — so for two people the shared tours are far better value.
  • Ravello day trip ~€40 for two ~A$68Travelmar ferry to Amalfi (€10, 25 min, 7 a day) then the bus up (€1.80). Villa Rufolo €8 — the garden terrace Wagner wrote about; Villa Cimbrone €10 — the Terrace of Infinity, statues on a parapet with a 300 m drop. Both open 09:00–20:00 on your dates.
  • CostieraSITA 24-hour bus pass €10 A$17Worth it from four bus hops. Write your name on it — it's nominative.
  • Emerald Grotto ~€10 ~A$17A flooded cave lit green from below, near Conca dei Marini. Bus from Positano, lift or stairs down, then a compulsory rowboat. Closes without warning in swell.
  • Cooking class €156 A$265Villa Genny & Emily up in Montepertuso — pick vegetables from the garden, make pasta and dessert, eat it with wine and limoncello. Max 6, runs to 15 Nov.

Beach clubs are already shut — Positano's pack up around 15 Oct. Budget nothing for sunbeds; the free sections of Spiaggia Grande and Fornillo are open, empty and pleasant. Boat trips cancel in swell, so book a day or two ahead once you can see the forecast.

Capri

20–22 Oct · 2 nights

Everyone comes for the day; almost nobody stays. Which means from about 5pm the island belongs to the few hundred people sleeping on it, and in late October that's a fraction of summer. Stay in Anacapri — quieter, cheaper, and the better half of the island.

⚠ Check your hotel is actually open Capri's winter closures start at the very end of October. 20–22 Oct is fine for most places, but confirm with the property directly before you pay.
Getting there — 20 Oct (the fragile link)

Hydrofoil Positano→Capri €27.50–2940–50 min. Use NLG, Coop Sant'Andrea or Lucibello — Alicost only sails weekends outside summer and 20 Oct is a Tuesday. Ferryhopper; confirm the sailing 2–3 days out, frequency drops sharply this late.

If the sea is rough €17.40–26Positano's pier is an exposed roadstead and is the first one dropped. Fallback: SITA bus to Sorrento (€2.60) then the year-round Caremar Sorrento→Capri ferry (€17.40). Three hours, always runs. Don't book anything unmissable for that morning.

Getting up from Marina Grande €2.40–17Funicular to Capri town €2.40 — but buses and the funicular only allow one carry-on-sized bag under 10 kg. A real suitcase needs a porter (€10–15) or the fixed-fare taxi (€17 for up to 4). Some hotels run a free port shuttle — worth choosing on that basis alone.

Do
✓ Buy the €12 combined ticket Villa Jovis + Certosa di San Giacomo + Casa Rossa on one ticket, valid a month. Separately they're €19.50 — this saves you €7.50 and it's sold year-round.
Blue Grotto — the cheap way: bus from Anacapri (€2.40) to the land entrance, then pay at the floating desk (€6 entry + €12 rowboat, cash only). Skips the €25 motorboat from Marina Grande. Roughly 50/50 open in late October — go your first morning, so day two is a retry. Skippers decide access at 09:00 📍€18
Monte Solaro chairlift — 13 minutes dangling over gardens to the island's summit, 589 m, with the whole Bay of Naples laid out. Walk down free through Cetrella if your knees allow 📍€14 rtn
Gardens of Augustus — the postcard view down to the Faraglioni and the switchbacks of Via Krupp. Best value on the island 📍€2.50
Villa San Michele, Anacapri — a Swedish doctor's house full of Roman fragments, with a pergola walk and a 3,000-year-old sphinx looking out to sea. October 09:00–17:00, open daily. Allow 60–90 min 📍€12
Villa Jovis — Tiberius's clifftop palace where he ran the empire for a decade. 45 min steep walk from the Piazzetta (no bus or taxi to the gate) — budget 2.5–3 h return. From 16 Oct it's 10:00–16:00, last entry 15:30, so arrive by 14:30. Card only. Closed Mondays 📍€6
Certosa di San Giacomo — 14th-century monastery, cloisters and a garden facing the Faraglioni, ten minutes from the Piazzetta and usually empty. October 10:00–18:00, closed Mondays 📍€10
Free walks — the Arco Naturale and Pizzolungo path, Via Krupp, Marina Piccola, the Migliara viewpoint from Anacapri (45 min, ends at a cliff edge nobody else finds) 📍free

Beach clubs are all shut by now — La Fontelina closed 11 Oct, Torre Saracena 14 Oct. Budget nothing for sunbeds; the walks and the views are the point.

Cefalù

25–29 Oct · 4 nights

Medieval lanes running down to a sandy beach, a vast rock behind the town, and a Norman cathedral with a gold mosaic Christ that has been looking down since 1131. Four nights here is the right call — one for Palermo, one for the rock, two for doing nothing. The clocks go back the day you arrive, so it's dark by six.

Getting there — 25 Oct (your train rides a ferry)

1 · Regional train to Villa San Giovanni ~€61h15–1h45 direct from Tropea, down to the toe of Italy.

2 · InterCity 723 to Cefalù €13–20Departs ~14:25. Here's the good bit: the whole train is shunted onto a ferry to cross the Strait of Messina. You can stay in your seat or go up on deck and watch Sicily arrive. Cefalù at 18:24. Book on Trenitalia — and re-check the time a month out.

DIY alternative ~€18–20Caronte foot ferry across (€2.50, every 40 min), walk 10 min to Messina Centrale, then a train along the coast. Same money, more flexible, less fun.

Money

  • Best setup: Up Bank debit (0% foreign fees, free overseas ATMs) as your main card, Wise as backup. Order both now — 1–2 weeks to arrive.
  • ATMs: use bank machines (Intesa Sanpaolo, UniCredit, BNL). Never Euronet — they're everywhere in tourist areas and they're the expensive ones.
  • The one rule: when the ATM offers to "convert to AUD", always say no and choose euros. That offer costs you 5–10%.
  • Cash: keep €200–300 on you. Needed for SITA buses, tabacchi, markets, small trattorias, porters, and the city tax at check-out.
  • Tipping: not expected. The €1–3 "coperto" on your bill is a cover charge, not a scam. Round up or leave €2–5 if you had a lovely time.
  • Daily food: ~€26 eating cheap, €42–57 with a trattoria dinner. Lunch is 30–40% cheaper than the same meal at night.

Safety & scams

These are safe places. The risk is petty theft, not anything worse, and the whole defence is keeping your bag in front of you in crowds. Official advice: Smartraveller.

  • Where to concentrate: Termini station, Metro Line A, bus 64, the Trevi crush; and in Naples, Garibaldi station and the Circumvesuviana train — that line has a real reputation. Bag zipped, in front, phone away from the doors.
  • Two scams to know: anyone tying a "free" bracelet on your wrist, and anyone with a petition clipboard. Both are distractions. Don't stop, don't take anything offered.
  • Taxis: official white cars only, meter on Tariff 1, or use the FreeNow app. From FCO it's a fixed €55 — check the door says "Roma Capitale".
  • Validate paper train and bus tickets in the little machines before boarding. €100 fine, and they do check.
  • Passport: carry it on Pompeii day (your ticket is named). Otherwise a photo on your phone and the real one in the room safe.

Packing

  • A 40–50L backpack or small soft case — not a big roller. Positano is stairs, Capri is stairs, and Italian stations love a broken lift. Ferries charge extra for big bags.
  • Pack a week of clothes and do laundry mid-trip — a self-service lavanderia is €14–20 wash and dry, or ask your hotel.
  • Layers: t-shirts, a light jumper, a packable rain jacket. Evenings drop to 12–15°C by late October.
  • Shoes matter here. Pompeii is uneven stone, La Rocca requires closed shoes, and Positano is a staircase. One pair of real walking shoes.
  • Swimmers and a quick-dry towel — the sea is 21–22°C the whole way down and the beaches are empty.
  • Hat, sunscreen, small umbrella, a scarf (doubles as shoulder cover in churches), two Type C adapters, and a refillable bottle — Rome has 2,500 free drinking fountains.

  • Boat around Capri €25–45Cheapest big-impact thing on the whole trip. Do it.
  • One sea-view night in Positano +€80–120Late October is the only time it's affordable. Waking up to that view is the memory.
  • Colosseum underground guided visit €32The tunnels where the animals and gladiators waited. Only €14 more than the standard ticket.
  • A cooking class €90–125Sorrento or Positano. You come home able to make it again.
  • Testaccio food tour, Rome €79Best possible first-day orientation, and you'll eat better all week for having done it.
  • Shared boat, Amalfi Coast from €50Seeing Positano from the water is the view the town was built for.
  • Three good dinners €40–50pp eachRoscioli in Rome, Le Grottelle on Capri, Lo Scoglio Ubriaco in Cefalù.
  • Hotel Kalura, Cefalù +€60/night ×4Private cove and a pool for the last four nights of a long trip. Worth it.
